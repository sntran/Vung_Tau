# Culture notes

This file lists what we know about the food and customs in the game.

Status values:

- **Confirmed**: I know it first-hand. It is correct for Vũng Tàu and the South.
- **Source**: a published source says it. I did not confirm it yet.
- **Check**: not sure. Do not use it in the game without my approval.

## Phở

| Fact | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| In Vietnam, phở is breakfast. Abroad, people eat it for lunch or dinner. | Confirmed | First-hand; [Hoc24](https://hoc24.vn/cau-hoi/viet-mot-doan-van-noi-ve-mon-an-nuoc-uong-ma-minh-yeu-thich-bang-tieng-anh-goi-y-mon-an-ma-ban-yeu-thich-la-gi-no-co-vi-nhu-the-nao.171435468365) |
| Many phở shops sell out by late morning. Some shops open from 5:00 to 11:00. | Source | [Foody: Phở Dậu](https://www.foody.vn/ho-chi-minh/pho-dau-nam-ky-khoi-nghia) |
| People order with the cuts in one phrase: "một tô phở tái nạm gầu bò viên". They do not only say "phở tái". | Confirmed | First-hand |
| Cuts: tái (thin raw beef, cooks in the broth), nạm (flank), gầu (fatty brisket), gân (tendon), sách (tripe), bò viên (meatballs). "Đặc biệt" means all cuts. | Source | [Seattle Times pho guide](https://projects.seattletimes.com/2016/food/pho-guide) |
| "Nước béo hành trần": a small bowl of fat from the top of the pot, with scallion. People put the onion in the bowl. They mix hoisin and chili sauce into the fat and dip the meat. You must ask for it. | Confirmed (name), Source (use) | First-hand; [Seattle Times pho guide](https://projects.seattletimes.com/2016/food/pho-guide) |
| "Chén trứng": an egg in its own small bowl. You must ask for it. | Confirmed | First-hand |
| How people eat the chén trứng (broth over the egg, or into the bowl). | Check | None |
| The herb plate (húng quế, ngò gai, giá, chanh, ớt) is part of the dish. Many visitors abroad do not use it. | Confirmed | First-hand |
| Many people put hoisin and chili sauce in a small dish to dip the meat. They keep the broth clean. | Confirmed | First-hand |
| Phở came South with Northern families after 1954. In the South, the broth became richer and the herb plate became bigger. | Source | [Vietcetera](https://vietcetera.com/vn/pho-goi-ghem-muon-vi-nhan-gian-trong-mot-bat-an) |
| Phở Bắc: clear broth, wide noodles, few herbs. Some Northern people do not want hoisin or sriracha in phở. Some Northern shops do not have bean sprouts or hoisin. | Source | [Vietnam Tourism](https://www.vietnamtourism.com/vi/bac-vs-trung-vs-nam-am-thuc-viet-nam-thay-oi-the-nao-tren-kh); [Foody review](https://www.foody.vn/ho-chi-minh/pho-dau-nam-ky-khoi-nghia) |
| You pay after you eat. You call "Cô ơi, tính tiền!". | Check | Prototype text |
| Tips are not expected at a quán. | Check | Prototype text |
| Prices: a bowl costs about 50.000₫ to 60.000₫. | Check | Placeholder |

## Coffee

| Fact | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|

## Vũng Tàu

| Fact | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| In the early morning, many people exercise at Bãi Trước. | Confirmed | First-hand |
| Fishing boats come in early. People buy fresh fish from the boats. | Confirmed | First-hand |

On the plate, they have shrimp floss on top. They come with mustard greens and herbs, pickled green papaya and carrot, and nước mắm with chili. Plates and bowls often have a printed flower pattern. | Source | Photos that I chose, 2026-10-24 |
| In the late afternoon and evening, many local families swim at Bãi Trước. Fishing boats stay at anchor in the bay. | Source | Photos that I chose, 2026-10-24 |

## Houses

| Fact | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Houses in Vũng Tàu are brick under plaster and paint. Brick shows only where plaster fell off, or on bare side walls that rise above a shorter neighbor. | Confirmed | First-hand |
| A tube house is narrow (about 3 m to 4 m) and deep. It has 2 to 4 floors. The ground floor opens to the street with a roll-up shutter or a gate. It is often a small shop by day and a garage for scooters at night. The roof is flat, with laundry and plants. | Source | [Local Vietnam](https://localvietnam.com/?p=92926) |
| Most walls are white, off-white, light grey, or cream. A few buildings, often mini-hotels and shops, have strong colors: salmon, turquoise, sky blue, yellow, or stripes. | Source | Photos that I chose, 2026-10-24 (not in the repository) |
| Many villas and some taller buildings have pitched roofs of terracotta tiles. Low buildings often have corrugated metal roofs. Flat roofs have stainless steel water tanks, satellite dishes, and antennas. | Source | Photos that I chose, 2026-10-24 |
| Thick bundles of overhead cables hang from concrete poles across and along the streets. | Source | Photos that I chose, 2026-10-24 |
| Near the beach there are many mini-hotels of 5 to 8 floors with vertical "KHÁCH SẠN" signs. | Source | Photos that I chose, 2026-10-24 |
| Ground floor fronts often have polished granite or ceramic tiles. Some fronts are fully covered with patterned tiles. Sidewalks are tiled. | Source | Photos that I chose, 2026-10-24 |
| Older women often wear đồ bộ (a matching printed shirt and trousers) and a nón lá, and ride bicycles with a basket. | Source | Photos that I chose, 2026-10-24 |
| In a dense hẻm, most roofs are corrugated metal (faded mint or teal, rusty brown, some red), between flat concrete roof terraces. Many terraces have stainless steel water tanks and solar water heaters. Villa areas have terracotta tile roofs. Schools and offices have long blocks with deep red tile roofs. | Source | Photos that I chose, 2026-10-24 |
| The walls are red hollow clay bricks (gạch ống) under the plaster. | Source | Photos that I chose, 2026-10-24 |
| The hẻm between dense houses can be only about 2 m wide. | Source | Photos that I chose, 2026-10-24 |
| The dry season is from November to April. The rainy season is from May to October. In the dry season, the hills turn partly brown and orange. In the rainy season, they are green. | Source | [MoMo](https://www.momo.vn/du-lich-di-lai/diem-den/ba-ria-vung-tau); photos that I chose, 2026-10-24 |
| Flame trees (phượng vĩ) bloom red around May and June, also on the road to the lighthouse. | Source | [SGGP, 3 June 2020](https://www.sggp.org.vn/phuong-vi-do-ruc-duong-len-ngon-hai-dang-post557108.html) |

## Daily life

| Fact | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Ăn sáng chưa?" (Have you eaten breakfast yet?) is a morning greeting. | Check | Prototype text |
| In the South, people use birth-order names. The oldest child is Hai, not Một. The youngest is Út. | Check | Prototype text |
| Many quán are the front room of a family home. A rolling metal shutter (cửa cuốn) closes the shop. | Check | Prototype text |
| Homestay meals are different from shop food. They include strong-smelling dishes and sauces. | Confirmed | First-hand. I will name the dishes. |
| Forms of address depend on age. Older people (about 50 and more) can call an adult traveler "con". A seller of about 40 does not: sellers call a foreigner "you", or "anh" or "chị". In this game, a seller of that age says "anh" to the traveler. | Confirmed | First-hand, 2026-10-24 |
| The player does not know the name of a person until they talk and the person is introduced. | Confirmed | My decision for the game, 2026-10-24 |
| Between people of about the same age, Southerners say "tui" (spoken "tôi"). It is friendlier than "tôi". | Confirmed | First-hand, 2026-10-24 |
| In the South, people can say "dì" where the North says "cô" for a woman of about the age of one's mother. Many people from the North live in Vũng Tàu, so both are heard. | Check | My memory, 2026-10-24. Not sure. |
| A younger person keeps the name that the neighbors use: "Cô Út" stays "Cô Út" for an older traveler. | Confirmed | My decision for the game, 2026-10-24 |

## Games for reference

- **Brother Hai's Pho Restaurant**: a Vietnamese indie game made with Godot. Players liked its true details of daily life, such as short stools and floor tiles. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brother_Hai%27s_Pho_Restaurant)
